Presence

Nonverbal signals, attention, posture, and first impressions.
1

The Flooding Smile

Pause long enough to register the person, then let a warm smile develop naturally so it feels directed at them rather than automatic.

2

Sticky Eyes

Maintain attentive eye contact and look away gradually instead of darting away; adjust intensity for culture, comfort, and context.

3

Epoxy Eyes

In groups, occasionally watch a person’s reactions as well as the speaker; use sparingly so attention never becomes staring or pressure.

4

Hang by Your Teeth

Use a simple mental cue to lengthen your spine, lift your posture, and enter a room with calm physical confidence.

5

The Big-Baby Pivot

When introduced, turn your whole body toward the person and give them complete attention for the first few moments.

6

Hello Old Friend

Approach a new person with the warmth you would naturally show someone you are genuinely happy to see.

7

Limit the Fidget

During important conversations, reduce distracting self-touching and restless movements so your attention appears steady.

8

Hans’s Horse Sense

Talk on two tracks: express your point while continuously noticing the listener’s face, posture, energy, and engagement.

9

Watch the Scene Before You Make the Scene

Mentally rehearse how you want to show up before a meaningful interaction: posture, tone, warmth, and pacing.

2

Conversation

Starting, sustaining, and deepening everyday conversation.
10

Make a Mood Match

Before leading a conversation, briefly match the other person’s energy and emotional tone so you meet them where they are.

11

Prosaic with Passion

Ordinary words can still feel engaging when your tone is warm, interested, and emotionally congruent.

12

Always Wear a Whatzit

At social events, carry or wear one distinctive, conversation-friendly detail that gives others an easy opening.

13

Whoozat

Ask a host or mutual contact for an introduction or a little context that can become a natural icebreaker.

14

Eavesdrop In

If you join a nearby conversation, do it transparently and politely by connecting to something you genuinely heard and can contribute to.

15

Never the Naked City

When asked where you are from, add one interesting detail that gives the other person something to respond to.

16

Never the Naked Job

When asked what you do, add a human or concrete detail instead of giving only a job title.

17

Never the Naked Introduction

When introducing two people, offer one shared interest or relevant fact so they are not left searching for a topic.

18

Be a Word Detective

Listen for repeated words, enthusiasm, and side comments that reveal what the other person actually enjoys discussing.

19

The Swiveling Spotlight

Keep shifting attention back to the other person; curiosity usually makes conversation feel more engaging than self-display.

20

Parroting

When you are unsure what to say next, reflect a key phrase from what the person just said and invite them to continue.

21

Encore!

In a group, invite someone to retell a story or insight you know they tell well, especially when it helps them feel included.

22

Accentuate the Positive

Early in a relationship, lead with constructive, energizing material rather than unloading every complaint or grievance.

23

Know the Day’s Context

Before an event, skim major current topics so you can follow the room and avoid obvious conversational blind spots.

3

Polished Communication

Clearer language, tact, confidence, and social fluency.
24

Skip the Abrupt “What Do You Do?”

Learn about someone through the conversation rather than making occupation the first or only way you define them.

25

The Nutshell Résumé

Prepare several truthful, short ways to describe your work, choosing the version most relevant to the person in front of you.

26

Your Personal Thesaurus

Expand your everyday vocabulary gradually so you can express ideas more precisely without sounding forced.

27

Delay the Quick “Me Too!”

When you discover a similarity, let the other person finish before jumping in with your own matching story.

28

Comm-YOU-nication

Frame requests and explanations in terms of the listener’s perspective, needs, and likely questions—not only your own.

29

The Exclusive Smile

Let your expression respond to the individual instead of wearing the same social smile for everyone.

30

Retire Tired Clichés

Replace stock phrases with plain, specific language that sounds like you rather than a script.

31

Use Vivid Language

Choose concrete, memorable wording and imagery when it helps an idea land more clearly.

32

Call a Spade a Spade

Use direct, accurate language instead of euphemisms that obscure what you really mean.

33

Trash the Teasing

Avoid humor that puts another person on the spot, especially before trust and boundaries are well established.

34

It’s the Receiver’s Ball

When delivering difficult information, consider how it lands on the recipient and pace the message accordingly.

35

The Broken Record

For intrusive questions, repeat a calm, brief boundary rather than overexplaining or becoming defensive.

36

Big Shots Don’t Slobber

When meeting someone impressive, stay respectful but grounded; admiration does not require fawning.

37

Never the Naked Thank You

Make gratitude specific: name what the person did and what it meant or changed for you.

4

Entering New Worlds

Learning enough context to connect across roles, interests, and cultures.
38

Scramble Therapy

Regularly sample unfamiliar activities, topics, and environments so you can connect with people outside your usual bubble.

39

Learn a Little Jobbledygook

Before entering someone else’s professional world, learn a few basic terms so you can follow the conversation respectfully.

40

Find the Hot Button

Ask enough questions to discover what matters most within the other person’s field, hobby, or concern.

41

Read Their Rags

Skim the publications, sites, or communities people in a field follow to understand what they are currently talking about.

42

Clear Customs

When communicating across cultures, learn local norms for greetings, eye contact, timing, distance, and taboo topics.

43

Bluffing for Bargains

Before negotiating, learn the market language and typical pricing so you can ask informed questions rather than pretending expertise.

5

Rapport

Creating a genuine sense of similarity, understanding, and shared experience.
44

Be a Copyclass

Gently match another person’s level of formality, tempo, and conversational style without mimicking them.

45

Echoing

Use some of the other person’s own preferred words and labels so they feel accurately heard.

46

Potent Imaging

Use sensory or visual language that fits how the other person naturally describes experiences.

47

Employ Empathizers

Replace empty listening noises with brief responses that name the meaning or feeling you are hearing.

48

Use Their Sensory Language

If someone describes things in visual, auditory, or feeling terms, respond in language that matches their frame.

49

The Premature WE

Once genuine rapport exists, inclusive “we” language can signal partnership—but do not force closeness before it is earned.

50

Instant History

Small shared moments, callbacks, and private references can create a sense of continuity in a new relationship.

6

Appreciation

Giving praise that feels specific, credible, and well-timed.
51

Grapevine Glory

Let sincere praise travel through appropriate third parties; people often trust appreciation they hear indirectly.

52

Carrier Pigeon Kudos

When someone says something kind about a third person, pass it along accurately rather than letting the compliment disappear.

53

Implied Magnificence

Ask for someone’s advice or perspective in an area where you genuinely respect their competence.

54

Accidental Adulation

A subtle, sincere compliment can be embedded in an ordinary sentence rather than announced dramatically.

55

Killer Compliment

Notice a specific, personal quality that is often overlooked and praise it sincerely rather than using a generic compliment.

56

Little Strokes

Offer small acknowledgments during everyday interactions so appreciation is not saved only for major moments.

57

The Knee-Jerk “Wow!”

When someone shares good news, respond to their excitement before changing the subject or analyzing details.

58

Boomeranging

Accept compliments gracefully, then reflect warmth back without immediately dismissing or arguing with the praise.

59

The Tombstone Game

Ask what quality someone most wants to be known for, then remember and reinforce that valued identity when it is genuinely present.

7

Phone & Remote Communication

Bringing warmth, clarity, and sensitivity to voice-only interactions.
60

Talking Gestures

Use vocal variety—pace, pauses, emphasis, and warmth—to replace the visual cues that disappear on the phone.

61

Name Shower

Use a person’s name naturally at key moments, especially greetings and goodbyes, without overusing it.

62

“Oh Wow, It’s You!”

Answer familiar callers with genuine recognition so the first seconds communicate that they matter.

63

The Sneaky Screen

If someone else answers, identify yourself and your purpose clearly rather than using deceptive tactics to bypass them.

64

Salute the Spouse

Acknowledge partners, assistants, family members, or colleagues who are important in the person’s life instead of treating them as obstacles.

65

What Color Is Your Time?

Before launching into a call, ask whether it is a good time to talk and respect the answer.

66

Keep Your Greeting Current

Use a short, professional voicemail greeting and update it when your availability or context changes.

67

Your Ten-Second Audition

Leave concise voicemail messages with your name, reason for calling, and a clear next step.

68

Sound Familiar, Not Entitled

If you know someone well, let that familiarity sound natural—but never use false closeness to pressure gatekeepers.

69

“I Hear Your Other Line”

If you notice an interruption in the background, acknowledge it and offer the person room to attend to it.

70

Instant Replay

After important calls, briefly review what was said, what was implied, and what follow-up you owe; record only when lawful and consented to.

8

Groups & Gatherings

Navigating rooms, social events, and group attention.
71

Munching or Mingling

At networking events, keep your hands free when you want to connect; eat first or step aside rather than juggling food through introductions.

72

Rubberneck the Room

Pause when entering a gathering, orient yourself, and scan calmly before moving toward a person or group.

73

Be the Chooser, Not the Choosee

Take appropriate initiative: approach people you genuinely want to know instead of waiting passively to be selected.

74

Come-Hither Hands

Open posture and visible hands tend to feel more welcoming than crossed arms, clenched fists, or a closed stance.

75

Tracking

Remember meaningful details people share and follow up later; continuity signals that you were actually listening.

76

The Business Card Dossier

After meeting someone, jot private, respectful memory cues about interests or follow-up—without recording sensitive details unnecessarily.

77

Eyeball Selling

Watch the listener’s nonverbal feedback and adjust your pace, detail, or topic instead of pushing through a fixed pitch.

9

Difficult Moments

Handling favors, conflict, mistakes, emotions, and social friction with tact.
78

See No Bloopers, Hear No Bloopers

Protect people from unnecessary embarrassment by not spotlighting harmless slips, spills, or awkward moments.

79

Lend a Helping Tongue

If someone’s story is interrupted, help them return to it by remembering where they left off.

80

Bare the Buried WIIFM / WIIFY

When asking for something, be transparent about what you gain and what the other person gains—or does not gain.

81

Let Them Savor the Favor

After someone helps you, express thanks and avoid immediately piling on another request.

82

Wait Before Calling in a Favor

Do not make generosity feel transactional by demanding repayment too quickly.

83

Parties Are for Pleasantries

Social gatherings are usually poor settings for ambushes, confrontations, or difficult negotiations.

84

Dinner’s for Dining

Meals work better for connection and constructive discussion than for surprise criticism or high-pressure conflict.

85

Chance Encounters Are for Chitchat

Do not corner someone during an accidental meeting to force a sensitive conversation; schedule it appropriately.

86

Empty Their Tanks

When someone is upset, let them finish fully before introducing your solution or counterpoint.

87

Echo the Emotion

Acknowledge the emotional experience before focusing on facts; people listen better once they feel understood.

88

My Goof, Your Gain

When you make a mistake, repair the practical harm and add a meaningful gesture that demonstrates accountability.

89

Leave an Escape Hatch

When correction is necessary, preserve dignity whenever possible and avoid humiliating people for minor wrongdoing.

90

Buttercups for Their Boss

When someone gives excellent service, tell their supervisor specifically what they did well.

91

Lead the Listeners

When you genuinely agree with a speaker, visible positive feedback can help create a safer, more responsive room.

92

The Relationship Scorecard

Notice imbalances in effort, favors, repair, and consideration; healthy relationships need reciprocity rather than entitlement.

Use these mini-prompts to turn ideas into behavior rather than trying to memorize everything.

Starting a conversation

You arrive early to a professional event and only know the host.

Try this

Scan the room, choose one approachable person, open with a context-based observation, then turn the spotlight toward them with a genuine question.

Hearing difficult feedback

Someone is visibly frustrated and you already want to defend yourself.

Try this

Let them finish. Reflect the emotion and the key point first. Only then clarify facts, ask what repair would help, and respond.

Making a request

You need a favor from someone who is already busy.

Try this

Ask whether it is a good time, state the request clearly, name what is in it for each person, and make “no” an acceptable answer.
